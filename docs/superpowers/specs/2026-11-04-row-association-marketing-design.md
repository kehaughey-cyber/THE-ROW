# THE ROW: Trade Association Marketing Workstream (Design Spec)

Date: 2026-11-04
Status: Draft for user review

## 1. Purpose

Build a trade association for right-of-way (ROW) infrastructure, starting with telecom and expanding to all ROW occupants. The first workstream is conventional marketing research, built backwards from the exit.

Not in scope here: the five-pillars and 7S frameworks (later), product build, legal entity formation.

## 2. Business concept (as stated by the founder)

**Products**
- Newsletter
- Semi-annual meetings
- Dashboard ("Bloomberg for ROW infrastructure"): infrastructure, spend, builds, projections. Differentiator: current, historical, **and predictive** data.

**Exit:** $300-500M valuation in 5-10 years, driven by subscriptions, memberships, and value-adds.

**Market:** ROW infrastructure. Telecom (fiber, cable, wireless/small cell, towers) is the beachhead. Electric, gas, water, and other ROW occupants are the expansion.

**Members (all three sides, from launch)**
1. Operators (telecom first)
2. Contractors and engineering firms
3. ROW managers (municipalities, DOTs, utility commissions)

**Commercial model:** two-sided subsidy. ROW managers join free or near-free in exchange for contributing data. Operators and contractors pay for membership, the dashboard, and the predictions.

**Positioning principle:** the association is a neutral convener, because the three sides have conflicting interests.

## 3. Assumptions to test (not yet facts)

- Exit is an acquisition (strategics such as data/analytics, trade-show, or media companies); value is a revenue multiple.
- A 5-8x revenue multiple implies roughly $40-100M ARR at exit. This range is a placeholder until the exit model tests it.
- Government data (permits, ROW inventory, fees) is obtainable at the scale and quality the predictions need.
- Operators and contractors will pay enough to carry the subsidized government side.

## 4. Approach

Hybrid. A first-pass ARR model sets hypotheses; each research document confirms or breaks one of them and the model is updated. Every claim is labeled **sourced**, **estimated**, or **assumption**.

## 5. Deliverables

| # | Document | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exit & ARR model | What must we reach, with how many members at what price? |
| 2 | Market definition & sizing | How big are the telecom beachhead and the wider ROW market? |
| 3 | Customer archetypes | Who are the operator, contractor, and ROW-manager buyers? |
| 4 | How they buy | Who decides, what is the budget cycle, what is the procurement path? |
| 5 | Competitive landscape | How do existing associations, data vendors, and permit platforms compare, and where is the gap? |
| 6 | Value proposition & positioning | Why join, why pay, why trust a neutral convener? |
| 7 | Membership & pricing tiers | What does each side get and pay? |

Order: 1 (first pass) then 2, 3-4, 5, 6, 7, with 1 revised after each.

## 7. Success criteria for this workstream

- Each document answers its question and labels every claim.
- The exit model shows the member counts and prices required to reach the target, and the research states whether they look achievable.
- A contributor who has not seen this conversation can read the repo and understand the market, the buyer, and the case.
