# Assumptions

## From document 02 (market sizing)
- The unique US telecom operator universe is 2,000-3,000 companies after removing overlap between NTCA, ACA Connects, WISP and municipal counts.
- About 20 national carriers and large cable MSOs, about 300 competitive fiber/co-op/other builders, and about 75 tower and small-cell infrastructure companies exist; none of these three counts was sourced.
- 40-60% of NAICS 237130 (power and communication line construction) establishments do telecom work.
- QCEW establishments convert to firms at a ratio of about 0.85.
- 2-4% of NAICS 541330 engineering-services establishments do telecom OSP, permitting, make-ready or ROW engineering.
- 5,000-10,000 of the ~38,840 counties, municipalities, townships, DOTs and commissions actively permit telecom work in the ROW.
- Fiber costs $1,000-$1,500 per home passed.
- About $21B of BEAD goes to deployment, spent over 3-5 years.
- ROW-specific costs (permits, fees, make-ready, restoration) are 5-10% of telecom capex.
- Gas distribution has 1,000-1,500 operating companies; about 10% of community water systems are large enough to pay for membership.
- 25-50% of water/sewer line and pipeline construction firms (NAICS 237110, 237120) are large enough or ROW-focused enough to join.
- 2-4% of engineering-services establishments do non-telecom utility ROW engineering.
- The expansion (electric, gas, water) adds 18,500-27,500 paying organizations, giving an all-ROW paying universe of 29,000-39,000.

## From document 01 (exit and ARR model)
- Most small operators and contractors cannot pay $30k a year; reaching the ARR target needs a top-heavy tier for the largest firms.
- Penetration above roughly 15% of a paying universe is unrealistic for a new association.

## From document 03 (archetypes)
- All archetype titles, budget owners, information sources and willingness to pay are hypotheses; no buyer interviews have been done.
- Permit-time variance is the shared pain buyers will pay to reduce; the evidence is operator advocacy filings.
- Large contractors value build and capex forecasts more than permit benchmarks.
- Large jurisdictions will pay a nominal fee in the NATOA range or join free in exchange for data.
- Utility commissions are a secondary audience rather than primary data partners.

## From document 04 (buying process)
- Private-company approval breakpoints (about $10k and $50k), dues vs data line-item ownership and sales cycles (1-12+ months by segment) are typical practice, not sourced.
- Membership priced above about $10,000 a year will be treated as a data/software purchase with procurement review.
- A free government membership is not a purchase and does not trigger purchasing thresholds; most large jurisdictions can approve it administratively.
- Agency-contributed data is likely a public record; the association will request only already-public fields and not pass operator-identifiable data to agencies.
- Operators should be signed before contractors in each region.

## From document 01 (price update after documents 03-04)
- A blended average of $5,000-$10,000 per paying member is realistic from published dues comparables; it is not validated willingness to pay.

## From document 05 (competitive landscape)
- Capabilities not described on a competitor's public pages were treated as not offered; demos and sample data could show otherwise.
- Unpublished enterprise prices (Shovels enterprise, CostQuest, Dodge, Irth, Accela, OpenGov and others) were not estimated and may differ widely from the dues and self-serve prices used.
- ROW managers will treat an operator- and contractor-funded association as neutral only if governance gives them real weight, as CGA's stakeholder board does.
- Cross-jurisdiction permit-time and fee benchmarks are a benefit ROW managers do not get from their own permitting software and would contribute data to receive.
- FBA, NATOA or a data vendor (Shovels, CostQuest) could add the missing pieces within one to two years.
- Shovels is the strongest threat to the dashboard and FBA the strongest threat to the membership.
- Trade-show and media companies are possible acquirers; no supporting transaction was found.
- The Ookla sale multiple (about 5.2x revenue) is a relevant comparable for a data business exit, not evidence of demand for this business.
