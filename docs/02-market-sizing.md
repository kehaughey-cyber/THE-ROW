# Market definition & sizing

## Question
How big are the telecom beachhead and the wider ROW market?

## Findings

### Side 1: Telecom operators (pay)

| Operator type | Count | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Rural ILECs / rural broadband companies (NTCA members) | ~850 | sourced [S-005] |
| Small and mid-size independent cable/broadband (ACA Connects members) | ~500 | sourced [S-006] |
| Wireless ISPs (WISPs) | ~2,000 | sourced [S-007] |
| Municipal broadband networks | 400+ | sourced [S-009] |
| National carriers and large cable MSOs | ~20 | estimated |
| Competitive fiber builders, electric co-op broadband, other | ~300 | estimated |
| Tower, small-cell and neutral-host infrastructure companies | ~75 | estimated |
| Sum of the rows (double-counts overlaps) | ~4,150 | estimated |
| Unique operator universe (overlap removed) | 2,000-3,000 | estimated |

- [sourced] NTCA describes itself as representing about 850 independent, community-based telecommunications companies [S-005]
- [sourced] ACA Connects has about 500 small and mid-size independent service-provider members, and NCTC nearly 700 (July 2025) [S-006]
- [sourced] Roughly 2,000 WISPs serve about 9 million US customers, per WISPA data reported November 2025 [S-007]
- [sourced] ILSR counts more than 400 municipal broadband networks serving more than 700 communities (September 2024) [S-009]
- [sourced] The national broadband map was built from data supplied by 1,731 providers, up from 1,650 at launch; the NTIA page is undated and the current filer count was not found [S-008]
- [estimated] The rows above overlap (many NTCA and ACA members also run fixed wireless; municipal networks file alongside co-ops), and the map's ~1,700 filers sits below the 2,000 WISP figure alone, so the unique operator universe is put at 2,000-3,000 companies, of which roughly 20 national carriers and MSOs account for most capex.
- [estimated] Most of the universe is small: NTCA, ACA and WISP members are typically single-market, family- or community-owned firms, so the number of operators able to pay $30k+ a year is likely in the low hundreds, not thousands.
- [sourced] WIA counts 154,800 cellular towers, 248,050 macro sites and 197,850 outdoor small cells in the US at end of 2024 [S-010]

### Side 2: Contractors and engineering firms (pay)

| Segment | Count | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Power and communication line construction establishments (NAICS 237130) | 13,191 | sourced [S-012] |
| Telecom-relevant share of 237130 (40-60%), converted to firms (x0.85) | 4,500-6,700 | estimated |
| Engineering services establishments (NAICS 541330) | 99,768 | sourced [S-013] |
| Telecom/ROW-relevant engineering firms (2-4% of 541330) | 2,000-4,000 | estimated |

- [sourced] BLS QCEW counts 13,191 private establishments and 239,681 employees in NAICS 237130 (power and communication line construction), 2024 annual average [S-012]
- [sourced] BLS QCEW counts 99,768 private engineering-services establishments (NAICS 541330), 2024 annual average [S-013]
- [estimated] NAICS 237130 mixes electric and telecom line work; a 40-60% telecom share and a 0.85 establishment-to-firm ratio give about 4,500-6,700 telecom-relevant contractor firms. Neither ratio was found in a source.
- [estimated] Only a small slice of engineering services does OSP, permitting, make-ready or ROW design; 2-4% of 541330 gives 2,000-4,000 firms. This is the least certain count in the document.
- [sourced] BLS QCEW counts 18,274 wired-telecom carrier establishments, which counts locations rather than companies and is used only as a cross-check on the operator side [S-014]

### Telecom paying universe

- [estimated] Operators (2,000-3,000) plus contractors and engineering firms (6,500-10,500) give a telecom paying universe of about 8,500-13,500 organizations. ROW managers are excluded because they carry zero ARR.

- [sourced] Major US broadband providers invested $94.7B in 2023; smaller providers, electric co-ops and satellite are excluded [S-002]
- [sourced] US wireless infrastructure spend was more than $63B in 2024, including construction, maintenance and operations [S-010]
- [sourced] Fiber passed a record 11.8 million US homes in 2025 [S-011]
- [sourced] BEAD totals $42.45B, allocated to states June 26, 2023 [S-003]
- [sourced] By February 2026 NTIA had approved 50 of 56 BEAD final proposals and estimated $21B of savings, about half of BEAD funding [S-004]
- [estimated] With about half of BEAD saved, roughly $21B goes to deployment; spread over 3-5 years that is $4-7B a year, and it sits inside the capex figures rather than on top of them.
- [estimated] The wireless figure overlaps the USTelecom capex figure and fiber build is part of both, so the telecom pool is about $95-110B a year, not the sum of the rows.
- [estimated] No source was found for the ROW-specific share (permit fees, make-ready, restoration). A 5-10% share of the $94.7B gives $5-9B a year; this is the slice the dashboard speaks to most directly.
- [estimated] The dashboard does not capture this spend; it sells information to the firms that spend it. The pool shows the stakes per member, not revenue.

- [sourced] EIA counted 168 investor-owned, 1,958 publicly owned and 812 cooperative electric distribution utilities in 2017, almost 3,000 in total [S-017]
- [sourced] NRECA represents more than 900 consumer-owned electric cooperatives [S-018]
- [sourced] EEI member utilities invested a record $178.2B in 2024 and project more than $1.1T for 2025-2029 [S-019]
- [sourced] AGA represents more than 200 local energy companies serving about 74 million of 79 million gas customers [S-020]
- [sourced] BLS QCEW counts 3,317 natural gas distribution establishments, 2024 annual average [S-021]
- [sourced] EPA counts over 148,000 public water systems [S-022]
- [sourced] EPA's service-area dataset includes over 44,000 community water systems [S-023]
- [sourced] BLS QCEW counts 12,958 water and sewer line construction establishments, 2024 annual average [S-024]
- [sourced] BLS QCEW counts 4,414 oil and gas pipeline construction establishments, 2024 annual average [S-025]
- [estimated] Gas operators are put at 1,000-1,500 companies (establishments overstate firms; a PHMSA operator count could not be opened). Water is mostly small municipal systems, so only about 10% (4,400-5,000) are treated as able to pay. Electric adds the non-telecom half of NAICS 237130. Together the expansion adds roughly 16,000-24,000 paying organizations, about double the telecom universe.
- [estimated] Electric is the largest expansion prize: its IOU capex alone ($178.2B) is about twice telecom's, and many public power utilities are also municipal ROW managers.

## Assumptions
- Counts of establishments are converted to firms at 0.85; no source was found for this ratio.
- 40-60% of NAICS 237130 is telecom work, and 2-4% of NAICS 541330 is telecom or ROW engineering.
- Association membership counts overlap; the unique telecom operator universe is 2,000-3,000.
- 5,000-10,000 jurisdictions actively permit telecom in the ROW.
- Fiber costs $1,000-$1,500 per home passed; BEAD deployment spend lands over 3-5 years.
- ROW-specific costs are 5-10% of telecom capex.
- About 10% of community water systems are large enough to pay; gas has 1,000-1,500 operating companies.
