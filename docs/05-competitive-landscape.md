# Competitive landscape

## Question
How do existing associations, data vendors, and permit platforms compare, and where is the gap?

## Findings

### How to read this document
- [assumption] Desk research dated 2026-11-04, from public web pages only: no demos, no sample data, no vendor or buyer interviews. Vendor capabilities are what each vendor advertises, not verified performance. "Does not do" means the page reviewed did not describe it; silence on a public page does not prove a competitor lacks the capability.
- [assumption] Weak spots: most vendors publish no prices (only Shovels, CTIA's report store and the associations' dues pages give dollar figures). CTIA, WIA, USTelecom and AGA do not publish dues. NATE's dues page, CGA's main site, S&P Global (Kagan) and Ookla's site could not be opened, so NATE dues other than one 2014 category, CGA's own description of its governance, and S&P and Ookla product details are not used. NRECA and EEI dues were not checked. The utility associations are expansion comparables only.

### Where the gap is
- [estimated] No organization reviewed combines all of: (1) members from all three sides with ROW managers as a full member class, (2) cross-jurisdiction ROW permit-time and fee benchmarks, and (3) a published, modeled forecast of builds and spend, sold as one membership. Each piece exists somewhere: three-sided governance and contributed data (CGA), ROW managers with industry associates (NATOA), permit-time intelligence (Shovels), modeled build forecasts (CostQuest), permitting benchmarks and cost studies inside an operator association (FBA).
- [estimated] The defensible part of the gap is a neutral, continuously updated ROW-process benchmark (permit time, fees, requirements by jurisdiction) that ROW managers help build and also use. Shovels covers permit records but has no government-side membership; FBA covers permitting but as an advocate for operators against fees, which makes it hard for ROW managers to treat its data as neutral [S-055]
- [assumption] Neutrality is only a differentiator if ROW managers actually contribute and trust the body. FBA's permitting study argues for fee caps and shot clocks against jurisdictions; a body that also sells to operators will face the same suspicion unless governance gives ROW managers real weight, as CGA's stakeholder board does.

### Where the idea is not differentiated
- [sourced] Prediction is not unique: CostQuest already forecasts fiber builds at location level [S-067]
- [sourced] Shovels markets itself as "The prediction engine for the built world" [S-062]
- [sourced] Shovels sells self-serve permit-data plans from $599 a month [S-032]
- [estimated] Prediction framing is not unique: Shovels already markets itself as a prediction engine and sells permit data at self-serve prices.
- [sourced] Permitting benchmarking inside an association is not unique: FBA commissioned a study of 100+ permitting examples across 71 local jurisdictions [S-055]
- [sourced] Forward-looking build-cost work inside an association is not unique: FBA and Cartesian published a cost-to-pass-90%-by-2029 estimate in 2019 [S-079]
- [sourced] Spend history is already published free by USTelecom (historical capex since 1996) [S-002]
- [sourced] CTIA already publishes annual wireless capex history and sells an indices report for about $1,075 a year [S-052]
- [sourced] A three-sided body with government members and a contributed-data dashboard already exists in damage prevention (CGA) [S-059]
- [sourced] NATOA offers an annual conference, webinars and a newsletter [S-030]
- [estimated] Newsletter and conference products are standard at the associations reviewed.
- [estimated] Incumbents could add the missing pieces quickly: FBA could license Shovels permit data or CostQuest forecasts and add a dashboard for its existing members; NATOA could add an operator tier; Shovels could add an events and membership layer. None needs a new organization.

### Strongest threat
- [estimated] The strongest threat to the dashboard is Shovels: it already sells telecom permit intelligence across 2,840+ jurisdictions at $7,200-$12,000 a year, uses "prediction" in its positioning and needs no government data contribution to grow [S-062]
- [estimated] The strongest threat to the membership is FBA: it already has operators and vendors paying $1,200-$9,500, runs research with Cartesian and RVA, and has made permitting a research priority. An FBA dashboard built on licensed permit data would take the operator side of the market before a new association signs members. This is a judgment, not sourced.
- [assumption] The combination most dangerous to the idea is an incumbent association partnering with a data vendor (for example FBA with Shovels or CostQuest). No such partnership was found; it is a scenario, not an observed event.

### Likely acquirers and strategics at exit
- [sourced] A data/analytics strategic paid $1.2B in cash for Ookla and related brands, which had $231M of 2025 revenue [S-070]
- [estimated] That is about 5.2x revenue ($1.2B / $231M), at the low end of the 5-8x range in document 01, so a $300-500M exit at that multiple needs about $58-96M of revenue. Ookla is a network-performance data business, not a ROW business, so this is a comparable, not evidence that buyers exist for this idea.
- [sourced] Private equity owns construction-data platforms: Clearlake Capital joined Symphony Technology Group as an equal investor in Dodge, a subscription construction-data company, on undisclosed terms [S-065]
- [estimated] Plausible data/analytics buyers are firms that already sell infrastructure data to the same customers: construction-data platforms (Dodge and its PE owners), permit-data vendors (Shovels), broadband-data providers (CostQuest), and network-intelligence buyers like Accenture. None has signaled interest; this list is inference from the comparison above.
- [sourced] B2B media groups already publish to this audience: Cabling Installation & Maintenance is part of Endeavor B2B's Digital Infrastructure Group [S-059]
- [sourced] Lightwave, which covered FBA's fiber research, is published by Endeavor Business Media [S-079]
- [assumption] Trade-show and media companies (Endeavor B2B, Informa, Questex) are possible acquirers of an events-and-newsletter business. No acquisition of a telecom or ROW association or show by these groups was found in this research, so this is an assumption.

## Assumptions
- Public pages describe what each competitor sells; capabilities not mentioned on the pages reviewed were treated as not offered, which may be wrong.
- Prices not published were not estimated; enterprise data contracts (Shovels enterprise, CostQuest, Dodge, Irth) may cost far more or less than the self-serve and dues figures used here.
- ROW managers will see an association funded by operators and contractors as neutral only if governance gives them real weight, as in CGA's stakeholder board.
- FBA, NATOA or a data vendor could add the missing pieces within one to two years; this timing is a judgment.
- Shovels is the strongest dashboard threat and FBA the strongest membership threat; both are judgments from public pages.
- Trade-show and media companies are possible acquirers; no transaction supports this.
- The Ookla sale multiple (about 5.2x revenue) is a comparable for a data business, not evidence of demand for this business.
